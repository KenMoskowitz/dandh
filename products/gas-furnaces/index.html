When a gas furnace makes sense in Tucson

While Tucson's winters are short, they're not trivial — especially at higher elevations or in homes that need fast, powerful heat when temperatures drop.

Colder elevations

Tucson-area communities above 2,500 feet — Catalina, SaddleBrooke, Catalina Foothills — see winter temperatures that make a gas furnace's fast, powerful heat generation particularly valuable.

Homes with existing gas service

If you already have a gas line, a gas furnace is often the most cost-effective heating solution. Natural gas is typically cheaper per BTU than electricity in Arizona, making operating costs favorable.

Paired with central AC

The classic combination: a central AC system for summer and a gas furnace for winter. Our split-system installations pair Daikin AC with the DM96HS furnace using a shared air handler for whole-home coverage.

Supplemental heat in colder years

Some homeowners choose a heat pump paired with a gas furnace as a dual-fuel system — the heat pump handles mild winter days efficiently, while the furnace kicks in on the coldest nights when gas heating is more economical.

D&H's Recommended Furnace

Daikin DM96HS

The DM96HS is Daikin's two-stage, multi-speed gas furnace — the system D&H most often recommends for Tucson-area homes that need reliable winter heating. At 96% AFUE, it converts nearly all the natural gas it consumes into usable heat, making it one of the most efficient furnaces available.

The two-stage operation means the DM96HS runs quietly at reduced capacity most of the time, only stepping up to full power on the coldest days. This produces more even temperatures, lower noise levels, and longer equipment life than a single-stage furnace.

Gas Furnace FAQs

It depends on the home. Central Tucson rarely sees temperatures cold enough to stress even an older heating system. But Tucson winters are colder than most newcomers expect — freezing temperatures occur most years, and homes at higher elevations (Catalina, SaddleBrooke, Catalina Foothills) can see extended cold snaps in the 20s. For those areas, a properly sized gas furnace provides reliable, fast warmth that a heat pump alone may not match on the coldest nights.

AFUE stands for Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency — it measures how much of the gas you burn is converted to usable heat. A 96% AFUE furnace like the Daikin DM96HS converts 96 cents of every dollar of gas into heat; only 4 cents escapes through the flue. An older 80% AFUE furnace wastes 20 cents of every dollar. The efficiency difference directly translates to lower gas bills every month the furnace runs.

A single-stage furnace runs at 100% capacity every time it turns on — like a light switch. A two-stage furnace (like the Daikin DM96HS) runs at lower output most of the time (first stage), only stepping up to full power on the coldest days. First-stage operation is quieter, more efficient, and creates less temperature swing. Your home stays more consistently comfortable, and the furnace runs longer cycles at lower capacity — which is better for both comfort and longevity.

A furnace replacement on an existing system is typically a one-day job. Our crew removes the old unit, installs the new furnace, connects it to existing ductwork and gas lines, and verifies full operation before leaving. For new construction or first-time installations requiring new ductwork, timing varies. Call (520) 408-2665 for a specific timeline estimate for your project.
